Refill Northbridge: Ending Single-Use Plastic Bottles on Campus
- ENV3010 Sustainability in Practice — Individual Presentation
- Candidate 0000
- Presented to the University Estates Committee
---
The Problem: Single-Use Plastic Bottles
- Around 410,000 single-use plastic bottles are sold on campus each year
- Fewer than half are recycled; many end up in general waste
- Only 14 working water refill points serve 22 buildings
- Conflicts with the University's 2030 net-zero and zero-waste commitments
Notes: Good morning. My proposal tackles a very visible problem on our campus: single-use plastic water bottles. Campus catering sales records show that roughly 410,000 bottles are sold every year across the cafés and vending machines. Our own waste audit suggests that fewer than half of these are recycled. One reason people buy bottled water is that refill points are hard to find: there are only 14 working fountains across 22 buildings. This sits uncomfortably with the University's public commitment to reach net zero and zero waste to landfill by 2030.
---
Supporting Evidence
- Campus waste audit (2025): plastic bottles are 9% of general waste by volume [1]
- Student survey (n = 612): 71% would use refill stations if more were available [2]
- Each bottle has a lifecycle footprint of about 83 g CO2e [3]
- Universities that installed refill networks cut bottle sales by 30–60% [4]
Notes: The evidence supports acting now. The Estates waste audit carried out last year found that plastic bottles make up about nine percent of general waste by volume. In the Students' Union sustainability survey, 71 percent of 612 respondents said they would use refill stations if more were available. Published lifecycle studies estimate around 83 grams of carbon dioxide equivalent per half-litre bottle, so 410,000 bottles represent roughly 34 tonnes of CO2e per year. Other universities that installed refill networks saw bottled water sales fall by between 30 and 60 percent.
---
The Proposal: Refill Northbridge
- Install 30 new chilled refill stations with bottle counters
- Give every new student a free reusable bottle at induction
- Phase out plastic bottled water in University-run outlets
- Add refill points to the campus map app
- Run a joint awareness campaign with the Students' Union
Notes: My proposal, Refill Northbridge, has five parts. First, install 30 new chilled refill stations, prioritising the library, sports centre and lecture buildings, with counters that show how many bottles have been saved. Second, give every new student a durable reusable bottle at induction. Third, once the new stations are in place, stop selling plastic bottled water in University-run outlets. Fourth, add all refill points to the campus map app so they are easy to find. Finally, run an awareness campaign with the Students' Union to build the habit.
---
Cost Estimate
- 30 refill stations installed: £2,400 each = £72,000
- Reusable bottles for 5,500 new students: £2.80 each = £15,400
- Awareness campaign and signage: £6,000
- Annual maintenance and filters: £9,000 per year
- Total first-year cost: about £102,400
Notes: Here is the cost estimate. Each refill station costs about £2,400 including plumbing and installation, based on supplier quotes, giving £72,000 for thirty. Branded reusable bottles for around 5,500 new students cost roughly £15,400. The campaign and new signage add £6,000, and maintenance and filter replacement will cost about £9,000 a year. The total first-year cost is therefore approximately £102,400, falling to around £24,000 a year afterwards if we continue the bottle giveaway.
---
Benefits
- Removes up to 250,000 plastic bottles a year from campus
- Saves around 20 tonnes of CO2e per year
- Free, chilled water improves student and staff wellbeing
- Visible commitment supports the University's sustainability ranking
---
Recommendations
- Approve the Refill Northbridge initiative and first-year budget
- Appoint an Estates lead and a Students' Union sustainability officer as joint owners
- Measure success using station counters and catering sales data
- Report progress to this Committee every six months
Notes: I have four recommendations for the Committee. First, approve the initiative and its first-year budget of about £102,000. Second, appoint an Estates lead and a Students' Union sustainability officer to own the project together, so that both infrastructure and behaviour change are covered. Third, measure success using the station counters and catering sales data, with a target of cutting bottled water sales by at least half. Fourth, ask the project team to report back to this Committee every six months.
---
Conclusion
- Single-use bottles are a visible, solvable problem
- Students want better refill options
- Refill Northbridge is affordable and measurable
- A practical step towards our 2030 commitments
